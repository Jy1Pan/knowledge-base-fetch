Where the other routes are strong

When an enterprise platform is the better choice

  • You already run a corporate data space (e.g. Catena-X) in production and need deep PLM/ERP integration from day one.
  • The battery passport is part of a larger ESG or traceability programme with its own integration team.
  • You need group-wide governance across many brands, plants and systems.

What Excel remains good at

  • As a data source: BOMs, serial numbers and material data are often already well maintained in Excel.
  • Batteriepasswerk starts exactly there - import a CSV or Excel file, and every row becomes a passport with its QR code.
  • Only the passport itself must not stay in the spreadsheet: it has to be electronically accessible via the QR code.
Frequent comparison questions

Excel, enterprise or SaaS - what manufacturers ask

Not as a standalone solution. EU Battery Regulation 2023/1542 requires the passport to be electronically accessible via a QR code on the battery - a spreadsheet alone does not fulfil that. Excel remains a good data source though: Batteriepasswerk imports CSV and Excel files and turns them into compliant passports with a QR code per unit.
When you run a corporate data-space strategy (e.g. Catena-X) in production, have your own integration team, and the battery passport is part of a larger PLM or ESG programme. For mid-sized manufacturers such platforms are often oversized - in budget and project duration.
Model-based software like Batteriepasswerk: maintain master data once per model, import serial numbers via CSV, roll out QR codes. Your first passport is free in the Pilot plan - no IT project, no setup fees.
No. The obligation is the digital battery passport under EU Regulation 2023/1542 - accessible via a QR code per unit. Catena-X is a data space for exchange along the supply chain: useful, but not a prerequisite. Batteriepasswerk is compatible; the Catena-X / AAS export is in preparation.
AI is only as useful as the problem it solves. Batteriepasswerk applies AI exactly where it saves time in the battery-passport process - such as OCR/AI-assisted data extraction from datasheets and supplier documents. Instead of a broad, general-purpose AI platform you get purpose-built automation for the mandatory fields of EU Battery Regulation 2023/1542, following the European Commission's official list of 71 data points (Version 2.0, 15 August 2026) - a faster route to a compliant passport, without the overhead.
For the battery passport, specialisation matters more than vendor size. Batteriepasswerk is built solely for the EU Battery Regulation: mandatory fields per the 71 EU data points, data structure and access levels aligned with DIN DKE SPEC 99100, GS1 Digital Link QR per unit, role-based access control (RBAC), an audit trail and EU hosting are built in, not bolted on. You get dedicated focus on exactly this legal obligation - without the complexity and project duration of a broad platform.
The obligation from 18 February 2027 is the digital battery passport - not a full supply-chain suite. Batteriepasswerk covers exactly that obligation end to end: a compliant passport with a QR code per unit, supplier self-service without an account, a readiness cockpit and a REST API for integration. Broader risk or ESG programmes can be valuable but are not a prerequisite for compliance - and can be added later.
